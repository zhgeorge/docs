Zero Hash provides two straightforward REST endpoints to request and execute quotes. You request a quote by specifying the instrument, side, and quantity; receive a firm price locked for 5 or 30 seconds; then execute by passing the `quote_id` back. The entire flow requires exactly two API calls to settle a trade.

<Note>
  Quotes can be configured with a default expiration of 5 or 30 seconds. Other expiration windows are available on request. All limits you define must fall within the constraints listed on the [RFQ Supported Assets](https://docs.zerohash.com/docs/supported-instruments-1) page.
</Note>

## Step 1 — Request a Quote

Call `POST /liquidity/rfq` to retrieve a firm quoted price for a given instrument and trade size.

- Specify the trading instrument (`underlying` and `quoted_currency`), the direction (`side`), and the size (`quantity` or `total`).
- Optionally include a `spread` value in basis points to apply a custom commission on this specific request.
- The response includes a unique `quote_id`, the all-in quoted price with fees embedded, and an expiration timestamp (`expire_ts`).

<Warning>
  Credit checks against participant balances are **not** performed on the RFQ request. Balance validation occurs only at execution time.
</Warning>

### Request Fields

<ParamField body="side" type="string" required>
  Direction of the trade. Must be `"buy"` or `"sell"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="participant_code" type="string" required>
  The participant code of the platform or customer on whose behalf the quote is being requested.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="account_label" type="string" required>
  The account label for the participant. Typically `"general"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="underlying" type="string" required>
  The asset or trading instrument being quoted. For example `"BTC"`, `"ETH"`, or a pair such as `"USDC.ETH"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quoted_currency" type="string" required>
  The currency in which the price is quoted. For example `"USD"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quantity" type="number">
  The quantity of the underlying asset to trade. Use `quantity` for asset-denominated orders or `total` for notional-denominated orders.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="total" type="string">
  The total notional value of the trade in the `quoted_currency`. Use instead of `quantity` for notional-based orders.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="spread" type="string">
  Optional. A custom spread in basis points to apply to this quote. For example `"25"` adds 25 bps to the quoted price. Overrides the platform default spread for this request.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quote_expiry" type="string">
  Optional. The desired quote expiry window, such as `"5s"` or `"30s"`. Defaults to the platform-configured value if omitted.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="fees" type="array">
  Optional. An array of fee objects to apply to this quote. Each fee object supports `name`, `type` (`"bps"` or `"notional"`), and `amount`. See [Spreads and Fees](/docs/spreads-and-fees) for details.
</ParamField>

### Response Fields

<ResponseField name="request_id" type="string">
  A unique identifier for this RFQ request.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="participant_code" type="string">
  The platform participant code associated with the quote.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="quoted_currency" type="string">
  The currency in which the price is denominated.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="side" type="string">
  The trade direction: `"buy"` or `"sell"`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="quantity" type="string">
  The quantity of the underlying asset covered by the quote.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="price" type="string">
  The all-in quoted price per unit of the underlying, including all spreads and fees.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="quote_id" type="string">
  A unique identifier for this quote. Pass this value to `POST /liquidity/execute` to settle the trade.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="expire_ts" type="integer">
  Unix timestamp in milliseconds indicating when the quote expires. You must call execute before this time.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="account_group" type="string">
  The account group associated with the quote.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="account_label" type="string">
  The account label associated with the quote.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="obo_participant" type="object">
  On-behalf-of participant details, including their `participant_code`, `account_group`, and `account_label`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="total_notional" type="string">
  The total notional value of the trade in the `quoted_currency`, including all fees.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="asset_cost_notional" type="string">
  The notional cost of the underlying asset, excluding spread fees.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="spread_notional" type="string">
  The notional value of the spread fee applied to this quote.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="spread_bps" type="string">
  The spread applied to this quote in basis points.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="underlying" type="string">
  The underlying asset or instrument being quoted.
</ResponseField>

## Step 2 — Execute a Quote

Once your customer confirms the quoted price, call `POST /liquidity/execute` with the `quote_id` from the active quote.

At execution time, Zero Hash performs two validations:

1. **Quote expiry:** The `quote_id` must still be within its validity window (before `expire_ts`).
2. **Balance check:** The customer account must hold sufficient balance to cover the full trade notional.

If both checks pass, the trade settles immediately and Zero Hash returns the trade details.

<Note>
  Credit checks are performed on quote execution to ensure the account can cover the trade. If the account has insufficient funds, the execution request will be rejected.
</Note>

### Request Fields

<ParamField body="quote_id" type="string" required>
  The unique quote identifier returned by `POST /liquidity/rfq`. This value must correspond to an active, unexpired quote.
</ParamField>

### Sample Request

```json
{
  "quote_id": "1f998343-d9f1-4b1d-bed7-df3aa8265bdb"
}
```

### Response Fields

<ResponseField name="request_id" type="string">
  A unique identifier for the execute request.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="quote" type="object">
  The full quote object echoed back from the original RFQ, including all price, notional, and spread fields.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="trade_id" type="string">
  The unique identifier for the settled trade. Use this with `GET /trades` to retrieve full trade details.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="status" type="string">
  The settlement status of the trade. A successful execution returns `"Completed"`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="trade_ids_list" type="array">
  An array containing the `trade_id`(s) associated with this execution. For standard single-fill trades, this array contains one entry.
</ResponseField>

## Step 3 — Retrieve Trade Information

After execution, retrieve full trade details using the `trade_id` returned in the execute response.

**Endpoint:** `GET /trades`

- Pass the `trade_id` to fetch details for a specific trade.
- The `/trades` endpoint also supports filter parameters such as instrument, date range, or `trade_id` for retrieving historical activity.

## Complete Worked Example

The following example shows a platform buying 500 USDC.ETH with a 25 bps spread applied.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request the quote">
    The platform calls `POST /liquidity/rfq` specifying `side: buy`, `quantity: 500`, `underlying: USDC.ETH`, and a `spread: 25` (bps).

    Zero Hash returns a firm price of `1.0025` per USDC (reflecting the 25 bps spread), a `quote_id`, and an `expire_ts` timestamp 30 seconds in the future. The `spread_notional` of `1.25` shows the fee embedded in the total notional of `501.25`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Present the price to the customer">
    Your application displays the all-in price of $1.0025 per USDC, with a total cost of $501.25 for 500 USDC. The customer confirms they want to proceed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute the quote">
    Before `expire_ts`, the platform calls `POST /liquidity/execute` with `quote_id: 1f998343-d9f1-4b1d-bed7-df3aa8265bdb`.

    Zero Hash confirms the quote is still valid, checks the customer account balance, settles the trade, and returns `status: Completed` along with a `trade_id`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the trade">
    The platform records the `trade_id` and optionally calls `GET /trades` to retrieve and display a trade confirmation to the customer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Quoting Controls

You can define boundaries and defaults that govern how quotes behave on your platform.

- **Minimum and maximum notional:** Set the smallest and largest trade sizes you accept. All limits you configure must remain within the constraints defined on the [RFQ Supported Assets](https://docs.zerohash.com/docs/supported-instruments-1) page.
- **Default quote validity:** Configure a platform-wide default expiry (5s or 30s). Individual requests can specify a different `quote_expiry` value.
- **Custom spread per request:** Pass a `spread` value in each RFQ payload to adjust commissions on a per-request basis, for example to run a promotion or differentiate by customer segment.
